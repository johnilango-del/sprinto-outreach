SPRINTO FIT RULES: WHICH COMPANIES TO EMAIL, WHO TO EMAIL, AND HOW

Source: the case brief (Term 2, AI Case 2, "Cold Outreach, at Scale") plus web research,
November 2026. Sprinto's sweet spot is a SaaS company getting its FIRST certification
(usually SOC 2) because bigger customers are asking for it. Sprinto is priced at the
budget end of the market, so it suits companies without a big security team.
Research sources: sprinto.com, trycomp.ai/when-to-get-soc-2, stmicro.net (SOC 2 guide),
iomergent.com/saas-security, nhimg.org/startup-security-roadmap, aitoolsatlas.ai (Sprinto review).

HOW TO JUDGE (the AI fills facts; plain code decides)
- Your job is to fill the FACT FIELDS below from the pages you were given. You do NOT
  decide whether there is "enough" information. Code checks which fields are filled,
  adds up the points, sets the status, and decides whether to fetch another page.
- Use ONLY evidence written on the company's own website pages that you were given.
- For every signal you use, quote the exact words from the page and name the page.
- No quote = no signal. Do not guess from the company's name, logo or industry.
- Never invent facts: no funding rounds, customer names or numbers that are not on the page.
- If a fact is not on the pages, leave its field EMPTY. An empty field is honest;
  a guessed field is a failure. Never fill a field just because pages ran out.
- The system fetches at most 4 extra pages beyond the homepage per company
  (see "WHERE TO LOOK" and "EVIDENCE MAP").

==================================================================
STEP 1: DISQUALIFIERS (any one = NOT A FIT, stop here)
==================================================================
1. Not a software company: agency, consultancy, services firm, hardware or physical product.
2. Sells only to individual consumers, never to businesses (no teams, plans for companies,
   or business customers anywhere on the site).
3. ALREADY CERTIFIED: the site names a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification, attestation,
   compliance badge, or report as already achieved. (Decision: any named certification
   puts the company out of scope; this campaign pitches the FIRST certification.)
   Code checks the RAW page text, including the footer, BEFORE cleaning, because
   certification badges usually sit in the footer.
   NOTE: "in progress", "coming soon" or "underway" is NOT a disqualifier.
   It is a STRONG signal (see Step 3).
   NEARBY-WORDS RULE: finding "SOC 2" or "ISO 27001" is not enough on its own. A heading
   can say "SOC 2 Type II" while the text says the audit is still underway. When the
   name is found, code checks the same sentence and the next two lines for in-progress
   words: "in progress", "undergoing", "underway", "pursuing", "working towards",
   "expected", "planned", "upcoming", "coming soon", "target", "Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 20xx".
   Any in-progress word = STRONG signal, NOT certified. Only a plain achieved claim
   ("certified", "compliant", "attested", "report available", "audited") disqualifies.
   If the page is ambiguous, leave certified EMPTY and FLAG it.
   (Found in testing: LogPulse's security page shows "SOC 2 Type II" as a heading,
   while its compliance page says the audit is still in progress.)
   NOTE: GDPR is a law, not a certification. "GDPR compliant" does not disqualify.
4. Too big: a public company, a household-name enterprise, or a site showing a large
   security organisation (a CISO plus a security team, a bug bounty programme, an
   investor relations page).
5. No real product yet: only a waitlist, a "coming soon" page, or a single landing page.

==================================================================
STEP 2: MUST-HAVES (both needed)
==================================================================
A. B2B software sold online (SaaS): customers sign up, log in, or buy plans for their team.
B. Handles customer or company data: the product stores, processes or connects to its
   customers' data (files, records, user accounts, integrations, payments, health or HR data).

If either is missing, leave the field EMPTY. Code fetches the next page, then sets NEEDS REVIEW (2A) or assumes yes and flags it (2B).

==================================================================
STEP 3: PRESSURE SIGNALS (is the company feeling the need NOW?)
==================================================================
STRONG signals (each one counts as 2)
- Security or Trust page says certification is in progress, planned, or "coming soon".
- Job ad for a security, compliance or GRC role.
- Named customers, logos or case studies from enterprises, banks, insurers, hospitals,
  governments, or other regulated organisations.
- An "Enterprise" plan, or wording like "for large teams", "SSO", "SAML", "audit logs",
  "custom security review", "contact us for security documentation".

MEDIUM signals (each one counts as 1)
- Healthcare, finance, HR, legal or education data mentioned as part of the product.
- Says it sells in the US, UK or Europe, or mentions GDPR or HIPAA.
- Funding announcement or "backed by" investors on the site (any stage).
- A Security page that exists but shows no certification at all.

STATUS (set by code, not by the AI)
- GOOD FIT: passes Steps 1 and 2, scores 2 or more points, stage confirmed.
- GOOD FIT, FLAGGED: passes Steps 1 and 2 but something is unconfirmed after the page
  limit (only 1 point, stage unknown, certification status unknown, or data handling
  assumed). Email is drafted; the flag shows on the Why card. (Two of three answered,
  one unknown = qualify and flag.)
- NOT A FIT: fails Step 1 or Step 2, OR scores 0 points after all pages are checked.
- NEEDS REVIEW: cannot tell whether it sells software to businesses (Step 2A) after
  the page limit. No email; a human looks.
- NO CONTACT: neither Hunter nor the Apollo list has a suitable person. No email.

STAGE: "Series A to C" is a guide, not a rule. A seed-stage company with a strong signal
can be a GOOD FIT. Size only disqualifies at the top end (Step 1, rule 4).

WHERE TO LOOK (code fetches the next page while a field is empty, in this order)
1. /careers or /jobs        (stage via open roles, security or compliance hires)
2. /pricing                 (enterprise plan, SSO, team plans)
3. /security or /trust      (certification status, security wording)
4. /customers or /case-studies (who they sell to)
Maximum 4 extra pages. /about is used only if it is linked from a fetched page
and a fetch is still left.

STAGE TEST (field: stage)
Answered when ANY one is on the pages: a funding stage named (seed, Series A, B, C),
5 or more open roles on the careers page, or an enterprise tier on the pricing page.

==================================================================
EVIDENCE MAP (one row per question; fields are filled by the AI, checked by code)
==================================================================
1. certified       | named SOC 2 / ISO 27001 cert, ACHIEVED (nearby-words rule) | raw footer, /security, /trust
                   | limit 1 | unanswered: treat as not certified, FLAG
                   | ends everything: YES (certified = NOT A FIT, stop)
2. b2b_saas        | product description naming a business buyer | homepage, /product, /about
                   | limit 1 | unanswered: NEEDS REVIEW
                   | ends everything: YES (No = NOT A FIT, stop)
3. handles_data    | product stores or processes customer data | pages already fetched
                   | limit 0 | unanswered: assume yes for software companies, FLAG
                   | ends everything: no
4. pressure_score  | strong (2) and medium (1) signals, each with a quote | /careers, /pricing,
                   | /customers, /security | limit 2 | 1 point: GOOD FIT, FLAGGED;
                   | 0 points: NOT A FIT | ends everything: no
5. stage           | funding stage, 5+ open roles, or enterprise tier | /careers first,
                   | then /pricing, /about | limit 2 | unanswered: GOOD FIT, FLAGGED
                   | ends everything: only at the top end (public or giant = NOT A FIT)
Worst case: 4 fetches beyond the homepage.

==================================================================
STEP 4: WHO TO CONTACT (Hunter first, the Growth Lead's Apollo list as backup)
==================================================================
ORDER: code looks for a contact ONLY AFTER a company qualifies (GOOD FIT or
GOOD FIT, FLAGGED). A company that fails Steps 1-3 is NOT A FIT, never NO CONTACT.

SOURCE 1, HUNTER (automatic): code asks Hunter's Domain Search for the people at the
company's domain, once per domain (results are saved and reused). Code, not the AI,
picks the person using this ranking:
  1. Head of Security, CISO, Security or Compliance Lead, GRC
  2. CTO, then VP / Head of Engineering
  3. CEO, Founder, Co-founder
  Never: sales, business development, marketing, growth, account, partnerships,
  interns, assistants, or generic inboxes (info@, hello@, support@).
  Within the same rank, pick the highest email confidence. Ignore emails under 70%
  confidence. Size check (from fit-rules Step 1-3 facts): at a small company, a
  founder outranks a VP of Engineering.
SOURCE 2, APOLLO LIST (backup): used only if Hunter returns nobody in the ranking,
is out of credits, or fails. The Growth Lead looked these people up by hand.
NO CONTACT: neither source has a suitable person.

The AI never invents, chooses or changes a contact. It only checks whether the
chosen title fits the company's size, and says so in the Why card.
The Why card always states the source ("Found by Hunter: CTO, top-ranked role found,
email confidence 94%" or "From your Apollo list: <the Growth Lead's reason>").
If the email domain does not match the company's domain, FLAG it.

==================================================================
STEP 5: PAIN POINTS (pick the TWO most likely for THIS person's role)
==================================================================
CTO / VP or Head of Engineering
- Engineers pulled off product work to answer security questionnaires and collect evidence.
- Owning security informally, on top of the day job, with no clear program.
CEO / Founder
- Enterprise deals stalled or delayed in security review.
- Certification has become a revenue deadline, with no one dedicated to it.
Head of Security / Compliance Lead
- Collecting audit evidence by hand across many tools.
- Small or one-person team facing an audit date and repeated customer questionnaires.
Each pain point must connect to at least one quoted signal from Step 3 for this company.

==================================================================
STEP 6: THE EMAIL (first email only)
==================================================================
- Address the person by first name. Sign off as "[Growth Lead name], Sprinto".
- Open with ONE specific hook taken from a quoted signal (for example, their "SOC 2 coming
  soon" line, their enterprise plan, or a named regulated customer). No generic openers
  such as "I hope this finds you well" or "I came across your company".
- Connect the hook to ONE of the two pain points, in their language, not compliance jargon.
- One sentence on Sprinto: helps SaaS teams get audit-ready for SOC 2 in weeks, with a
  dedicated compliance expert, without pulling engineers off product.
- One soft ask: a short call, or "worth a look?". No pressure, no fake urgency.
- Under 120 words. Plain text. No emojis, no exclamation marks.
- Every fact about their company must come from a quoted page. If unsure, leave it out.

==================================================================
OUTPUT (what to return for every company)
==================================================================
- Fact fields: certified, b2b_saas, handles_data, pressure signals, stage
  (each with the exact quote and the page it came from, or EMPTY)
- Reasons: each rule or signal used, with the exact quote and the page it came from
- (Code then adds up the points and sets the status.)
- Contact check: does the chosen title fit the company's size? (yes / no, one line)
- Two pain points, each linked to a quoted signal
- The email (only for GOOD FIT or GOOD FIT, FLAGGED)
